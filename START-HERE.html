How you work with this

  1. In the morning say "good morning". Claude fetches calendar and mails, briefs you and builds this dashboard. Optionally you plan the day through afterwards.
  2. During the day just talk, no command needed: "chapter 3 is done", "waiting on IT", "meeting went well, we are doing X". Claude files it away.
  3. Documents go into the inbox/ folder, then say "read this in". Decisions and to-dos land in the right project.
  4. In the evening "end of day": a short wrap-up, what happened and what is left over. What is coming up in the next few days you simply ask in the chat ("what is on this week?").
  5. If something is wrong, just say so: "that is not right", "I answered that long ago". It gets corrected, no discussion. If the same mistake comes up repeatedly, the cause gets fixed.
  6. In a hurry? "Good morning, quick" skips the inbox analysis: calendar and tasks in about 30 seconds. Been away longer? Just say "good morning", you will be asked what piled up.

You do not have to remember any commands. "What is on today?" does the same as "good morning", and one fresh conversation per day is plenty.

The setup, step by stepfour phases, twenty to thirty minutes, and you can stop after any one of them

You answer a few questionsabout five minutes

  1. Which language you wantEverything the system says to you afterwards follows it.

    Briefing, dashboard, the entries in your files, mail drafts. The package's own files stay English; that is a different thing and does not concern you.

  2. Fresh start, or a folder you already haveDecides which of two different jobs this is.

    If you already have a folder with your work in it, nothing new gets built next to it, that one gets taken over. Skipping this question is how people end up with two half systems and neither one complete.

  3. Six questions about youName, work, location, projects, the tools you use daily, your plan.

    The tools question is the one that pays off later: your systems get connected by name further down, instead of being asked about abstractly fifteen minutes after you already said it. And the plan question decides how generously the system works, because you know your plan, it is on your invoice.

It sets itself upnothing asked, it just runs

  1. It writes itself up from your answersYour settings, your model, a quiet look at your machine.

    Three things at once, and none of them needs you. Everything you said lands in one config file that every command reads from afterwards. The model gets worked out from your plan rather than asked about: on the twenty-euro plan always the fast one, because the expensive one burns through that quota in an hour or two. And it checks quietly what this machine can do, which connections already answer and whether the dashboard can be built here at all, so nobody has to ask you twice.

  2. Your projects become foldersOne per project, each with what it is and where it stands.

    That page is the difference between a folder of files and something that can answer where everything stands on a Monday.

  3. Whatever documents you broughtif you have anyFiled into the right project, not onto a heap.

    And what is inside them gets pulled out on the way: decisions, open points with an owner, the people involved.

It connects your worldthe part that otherwise never happens

  1. Your writing style, read oncemailasked firstSo drafts sound like you, not like a template.

    Your own sent mail is read once, read only, nothing is ever sent, and you say yes before it happens. Without this step every draft still works, it just sounds generic.

  2. The tools, the plugins, two accountsCLIplaywright and firecrawl, eight plugins, and the keys they need.

    Three things in one step, because they only work together: a tool without its key does nothing, and a key without its tool has nothing to unlock.

    playwright
    A real browser Claude can drive. Log into a portal, fill a form, check whether a page actually looks right after a change. This is what turns "I cannot see that" into "I looked".
    firecrawl needs a key
    Reads web pages and searches, without opening a window. A competitor's site, a supplier's price list, what changed in a regulation. Your own free account, so nobody shares a limit with you.
    Eight plugins
    The curated set, listed one by one in the first card below. New ones appear almost daily and telling this week's real thing from the noise is a job of its own — handing you a list to install yourself would give you back exactly the work you were meant to be spared.
    OpenRouter a key
    Images, and models that do not come from Anthropic. Only matters if you generate visuals or want a second opinion from a different model.

    Where the keys live: in a file outside this folder, with permissions only you can read. That way a repo that gets cloned, backed up or shared never carries them.

  3. Your systems, connected by nameconnectorMailbox, calendar, and whatever else you named.

    Connecting happens once, in Claude Cowork under Settings, and Claude Code then uses the same connection. Read access only, and the two promises hold everywhere: nothing is ever sent and nothing is ever written into your calendar.

    Your mailbox not optional
    Outlook, Gmail, Microsoft 365, Google Workspace. Without it the morning briefing cannot tell what needs an answer from what is just noise, and no draft can be written at all.
    Your calendar not optional
    Appointments in the briefing and the day timeline, including the gaps between them. Reminders are shown apart from real meetings, so a to-do does not masquerade as an appointment.
    Where your customers live
    HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive. Then "where do we stand with them" is answered before a call instead of during it, and a draft carries the real history rather than your memory of it.
    Where the work is tracked
    Linear, Jira, Asana, ClickUp, Notion. Read as context, deliberately never as a second task list: your own open points stay in one place, otherwise you have two lists and neither one is right.
    Where your files are
    SharePoint, OneDrive, Google Drive. A document gets filed straight from where it already lives, instead of being downloaded first and forgotten in a folder afterwards.
    Where the talking happens
    Teams or Slack. What gets agreed in a thread at four in the afternoon otherwise never reaches any project.
    Where your code is
    GitHub, Supabase, Vercel. Only comes up if you build software; skipped without comment if you do not.

    Not in the catalogue? Then that system's own connector gets added directly instead. You fetch a token, Claude runs the command. It only ends at "no way in" once that has actually been checked.

  4. Git: your code in, your state outrepoYour projects attached, your own private backup created.

    Two directions. A project with its own repository gets cloned into that project keeping its full history, nothing copied, nothing merged. And a private repo gets created for you under your own account, visible to nobody else, where the end of the day pushes your state so a lost laptop is not a lost year.

8The plugins, one by onestep 9, and what each one is actually for
  • skill-creatorYou describe something you do every week; it builds you your own command for it. This is the one that turns the folder from a fixed set of features into something that grows with you.
  • ponytailKeeps whatever gets built small. Without it an AI answers "add a cache" with a cache class; with it, with one line. Matters most for people who do not read code and cannot tell the difference.
  • claude-code-setupReads how you have actually been working after a few weeks and says which of your repeated steps is worth turning into a command. Useless on day one, valuable in month two.
  • code-reviewReviews your own changes before they go out: several viewpoints first, then a separate pass that throws out everything it cannot actually prove. Pairs with /security-review, which Claude Code brings itself.
  • claude-md-managementYour rules file grows every time you say "from now on". After months it sprawls and starts contradicting itself. This tidies it.
  • impeccableDesign guidance for anything with a screen: layout, spacing, colour, accessibility. Only pays off if you actually build interfaces.
  • superpowersDevelopment method: test first, debug systematically, plan before writing. For the people on your team who write code.
  • claude-memRemembers across sessions, so a new chat does not start from nothing. The one that gets asked about first: it keeps running in the background and writes down what it reads, including client documents. Yes or no, both are fine, everything works without it.
